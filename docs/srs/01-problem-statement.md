# 01 — Problem Statement

## The problem

Citizens across Sri Lanka encounter everyday civic issues in their neighbourhoods:

- Illegal garbage dumping on roadsides and vacant land
- Broken or non-functioning streetlights
- Hazardous potholes on local roads
- Burst or leaking water pipes
- Blocked drains that pool stagnant water — a dengue-breeding risk

These are the responsibility of local government bodies — Pradeshiya Sabhas (Divisional Councils), Urban Councils, and Municipal Councils — but the reporting process is almost entirely manual and offline:

1. A resident notices the issue.
2. To report it, they must physically visit the relevant council office during working hours, or track down the right officer's contact number.
3. They file a written complaint or make a verbal report, with no reference number and no way to check on it later.
4. The council has no centralized digital record of what's been reported, what's been assigned, or what's been fixed.
5. Urgent issues (e.g. a garbage pile near a residential area breeding mosquitoes) get no priority over minor ones — everything is triaged manually, if at all, by whoever reads the complaint book.

## Who is affected

- **Residents**, who have no low-friction way to report a problem and no visibility into whether it's being addressed — leading many to simply stop reporting.
- **Municipal engineers and council staff**, who have no shared system to track incoming issues, assign work, or measure backlog — everything lives in paper files or informal notes.
- **The wider community**, who bear the health and safety cost of slow response — stagnant water and dengue risk, road accidents from unrepaired potholes, uncollected waste.

## Why this matters now

Urban and semi-urban population density in Sri Lanka continues to strain municipal services that were never digitized. Dengue outbreaks are a recurring seasonal public-health issue directly linked to slow response to drainage and waste complaints. A low-cost, easy-to-deploy digital reporting layer — even a simple one — closes a real, current gap between residents and the councils meant to serve them.

## Evidence this is a real, current, local problem (cite in the demo/PDF)

- Recurring dengue case surges reported each rainy season, frequently linked in public health advisories to blocked drains and uncollected waste.
- Local news coverage of unresolved pothole and streetlight complaints in multiple municipal areas.
- Absence of any unified national or municipal digital complaint-tracking system for civic infrastructure issues (unlike, e.g., utility billing, which is digitized).
